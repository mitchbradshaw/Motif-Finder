# B10: at which time scales does structure beyond noise appear, per recording? 2026-11-03

Round-B question B10, thesis Chapter 10 (`10_roundb.tex`, "Time scales and methods from other fields"). Two sources:
the `motif_lengths` template run on the website (`scripts/thesis/b10_motif_lengths_site.py` posts each chain to the
bridge's `/api/runs`, the route the Analyse page's Run button uses, one at a time), and the CNN templates' existing
real-against-surrogate counts (`scripts/thesis/b10_cnn_scales.py`, read-only). M4_aug was never read; S. commune was
never run, viewed or exported.

## In plain words

Imagine listening to a recording and asking: "At what tempo does something repeat that a random hum of the same
pitch would not?" The two tools here ask that at several tempos (time scales) at once.

- **`motif_lengths`** checks window lengths from 30 s to 1 h. For each, it asks whether the recording holds closer
  look-alike pieces than shuffled copies with the same "hum" (AAFT) do.
- **The CNN templates** look at 1, 10 and 60 min windows. They ask whether the network finds more events in the real
  recording than in one phase-randomised copy.

**Answer: structure beyond noise appears at seconds-to-minutes scales on four of five recordings. The strongest
evidence is always at the shortest length (30–46 s), and nothing is judged past about 25 min on 8 h spans. The
exception is MJu26a.**
- **M2_aug (oyster), CO and LM:** `motif_lengths` clears from 30 s up to 16–25 min.
- **M2_concat:** it clears from 30 s up to 4.4 min.
- **MJu26a:** no length clears. Its 8 h span is a steady oscillation with a period of about 20 min, and the
  AAFT copies keep exactly that rhythm (they keep the spectrum), so nothing repeats beyond them.
- **CNN, 10 min:** the clearest scale. GADF and GASF find 1.3–10× more events than the copy on every recording; the
  oyster and lion's-mane M2 recordings are strongest (4.5–10×).
- **CNN, 60 min:** a few encodings exceed the copy on CO, M2_aug and MJu26a (as L_LM).
- **CNN, 1 min:** below the copy almost everywhere (ratio about 0.35), because the network calls more 1 min events in
  noise than in the recordings.

So the two tools agree that minutes-long shapes beat noise. They disagree at the shortest scale, where the matrix
profile finds its strongest gap and the CNN finds noise more "eventful" than the real signal.

**Recommendation:** state B10 per recording as a band, not a single preferred scale:
- seconds to tens of minutes on M2_aug, CO and LM;
- seconds to a few minutes on M2_concat;
- nothing beyond its own rhythm on MJu26a.

Also state that `motif_lengths`' "strongest gap at 30 s" is the bottom of the tested range, not an event length.

## Protocol (declared before any result was read)

### `motif_lengths` on the site

- **Chain:** the shipped template unchanged: a 2 h rolling-mean detrend, then the pan matrix profile at 12 window
  lengths from 30 s to 1 h, each length judged against 3 AAFT copies of the span (the block's own null, seed 0).
- **Spans:** channel 0 of each recording, one 8 h span starting at 50 % of (length − 8 h). A span more than 10 % NaN
  would move to the next channel (none did). For the two longest recordings (M2_aug, MJu26a) a second 8 h span at
  25 % was planned "if time allows".
- **Reading:** for each judged length, whether it clears its null (the block's `clears` field), and its gap; the band
  of cleared lengths; the strongest-gap length (which the block itself says is not an event length). On an 8 h span a
  length is judged only up to about 34 min (the span must hold 14 windows), so the 45 min and 1 h rows are
  "too long to judge" by construction.

### CNN templates at 1, 10 and 60 min (existing data)

- **Source:** the `cnn_*` rows of `docs/thesis/data/detectors_vs_surrogates/by_variant_recording.csv` (the RQ4b
  sweeps: every channel of a recording, each run on the real channel and on one phase-randomised copy).
- **Recordings:** M2_aug, M2_concat, CO_fs2, LM_fs2, and L_LM_fs2 standing in for MJu26a (the sweep ran the five-channel
  L_LM copy, not MJu26a itself). L_LM_10Hz is dropped: the same channels again.
- **Rule:** a (encoding, scale, recording) cell "exceeds its surrogate" when the real detections outnumber the copy's
  (ratio > 1) **and** more than half the channels do so individually. A scale shows structure on a recording when at
  least half of its four encodings (fusion3, GADF, GASF, recurrence) exceed.
- **Caveat declared up front:** one phase-randomised draw tests response to the spectrum's phases, with a Gaussian
  marginal; Chapter 6 found such ratios stable over 20 draws, but this is not the AAFT null `motif_lengths` uses.

**Reading.**
- On four of five recordings, every judged length from 30 s up to the band's top clears. The band is one unbroken
  run starting at 30 s, which is the "band picks no window" pattern W3 reported.
- The gap mostly grows towards short lengths, so the strongest gap sits at or next to the shortest rung. The block
  itself says this is not an event length: the shortest windows repeat as pieces of anything longer.
- The top of the band differs by recording: about 4 min on M2_concat, 16 min on M2_aug and LM, and 25 min on CO,
  where the 25 min gap (16.6) is the second largest. CO's spans hold slow repeated shapes that M2_concat's do not.
- **MJu26a** is the opposite case. Its 8 h span is a regular oscillation with a period of about 20 min, some 23 cycles in 8 h (see the
  screenshot's trace). AAFT copies keep the spectrum, so they keep the oscillation, and the recording's look-alikes
  are no closer than the copies'. All gaps are below 2. This says the span is its rhythm and nothing more; it does not
  say the span is empty.

  the CNN's "event" is something phase-randomised noise produces more readily than the real signal does.

### Both sources together, per recording

| Recording | `motif_lengths` (AAFT, 8 h) | CNN (phase copy) | Scales with structure beyond noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| M2_aug | 30 s – 16 min | 10 and 60 min | seconds to about an hour |
| M2_concat | 30 s – 4.4 min | 10 min (60 min not swept) | seconds to about 10 min |
| CO_fs2 | 30 s – 25 min | 10 and 60 min | seconds to about an hour |
| LM_fs2 | 30 s – 16 min | 10 min | seconds to tens of minutes |
| MJu26a | none (span is a ~20 min oscillation) | 10 and 60 min (as L_LM) | only through the CNN, against a phase copy |

On MJu26a the two disagree, and the nulls explain it. The phase copy keeps the oscillation's spectrum but not its
non-Gaussian shape, so a CNN can tell the real cycles from the copy's. The AAFT copy keeps the values and the
spectrum, and the matrix profile finds the copy's cycles just as close as the real ones.

## Caveats

- **One 8 h span per recording, channel 0.** The planned second spans (M2_aug and MJu26a at 25 %) were cut for time:
  the runner was stopped after the MJu26a 50 % job, and job 840 was recorded by hand (`b10_finish_job.py`). A
  recording's band can change from span to span, and the MJu26a result is one channel and one 8 h stretch.
- **8 h judges only up to about 25 min.** The two longest rungs (38.8 min, 1 h) are never judged. Structure at 30–60
  min, which the CNN's 60 min scale touches, needs 24 h `motif_lengths` spans (about 516 s each when the machine is
  quiet).
- **The two nulls differ.** `motif_lengths` uses 3 AAFT copies; the CNN rows use one phase-randomised copy. Chapter
  6 found such CNN ratios stable over 20 draws, but a phase copy also Gaussianises the values. B9 showed that this
  alone can make a detector "beat" it, so the CNN's 10 and 60 min margins are an upper bound on structure beyond AAFT.
- **MJu26a in the CNN rows is L_LM_fs2:** five MJu26a channels (×1000 plus an offset), not channel 0 of MJu26a.
- **"Strongest gap" is not an event length** (the block's own rule). It sits at the shortest rung on four of five
  spans.
- `spike_event_features`, which the chapter note names, was not run (time).
