## In plain words

The FitzHugh–Nagumo (FHN) model is the simplest textbook "neuron": two variables, a fast one that behaves like the
voltage and a slow, hidden one that acts like a recovery variable. Set up one way it ticks forever, like a metronome
(the **oscillatory** regime). Set up another way it sits still until something kicks it, makes one big swing and
settles again, like a mousetrap that resets itself (the **excitable** regime). We asked whether the average shape of a
fungal drop cycle looks like either, by bending the model to fit the recorded shape as closely as it can.

A good fit is only interesting if noise cannot be fitted as well. So every fit was repeated on "fake cycles": stretches
of the same recording where nothing happens, cut and averaged the same way, and copies of the recording with its timing
scrambled but its frequency content kept. A model that fits the fakes as well as the real thing has told us nothing.

**The answer is no, not yet.** On the two oyster sequences the model fits well (R² 0.87–0.98), but it fits the quiet
fake cycles just as well (their best R² 0.98–0.99), and a simpler non-neuron shape — a sudden fall followed by a smooth
recovery, the kernel the app's OASIS detector already uses — fits the real cycles better than either FHN regime. Of the
two FHN regimes, the excitable one (one kicked swing from rest) fits the oyster cycles better than the oscillating one.
On the reishi sequence FHN is the best model and beats every fake by a hair, but that mean cycle is close to a smooth
wave, and FHN's advantage over a plain sine wave is no larger than on the fakes. What the real cycles do have, and the
fakes lack, is **size**: the real mean cycles are 4–12 times taller.

The phase-response pictures (how much a small voltage nudge would advance or delay the next cycle) are pictures of **the
fitted model only**. Nobody nudged a fungus. And, as the paper's central lesson says, even the model's answer depends on
which hidden variable we pretend stays fixed during the nudge: holding the delayed voltage fixed instead of the recovery
variable changes the curve by a factor of ten or more.

## What was fitted

The mean of each sequence's cycles (onset to next onset, the drop placed at 10 % of the cycle, chord-detrended as
`interrogation.cycle_shapes` does, 200 points; cycles outside 0.6–1.6× the median gap left out). Events are the
drop-motif store's rows; counts match the `sequences` table for all three. Units from `recordings.units` (all three
`V`, ×1000 to mV).

Models (all with an exact gain and offset; k counts every free parameter):

* **FHN oscillator** (k = 7): the limit-cycle voltage, time ratio (model cycles per recorded cycle), phase shift.
  Parameterised by the fixed point so every trial set oscillates.
* **FHN excitable** (k = 8): rest at a stable fixed point, an instantaneous voltage kick, one excursion.
* **jump + exponential** (k = 5) and **jump + exponential with rise** (k = 6, a difference of exponentials): the
  OASIS kernel of `detection.deconvolve`, periodic.
* **one sinusoid** (k = 3): the "it is just periodic" baseline.

**Identifiability.** With the gating variable hidden, `a` and `z` enter the voltage only through α = a + b·z (tested:
`test_a_and_z_enter_v_only_through_a_plus_bz`). The fits report α; `a` is printed with z fixed at the paper's −0.4.

## The PRC figures (model only)

`seq<N>_fig1_style`: the fitted limit cycle in the (w, v) plane with isochron ticks coloured by phase, and the voltage
PRC with (green) w held fixed, (orange) the delayed voltage held fixed at the paper's delay of 0.1 model units, (violet)
at a quarter-period delay, (blue) a linear change of hidden coordinate. `seq<N>_fig4_style`: ground truth, simulated
noisy model observations and their rolling median, constant delay and corrected curves, residuals. Converted to
recorded units the w-fixed PRCs peak at 0.29 rad/mV (seq 3), 6.2 rad/mV (seq 7) and 29 rad/mV (seq 22).

Checks: the perturbation PRC and the adjoint agree to 0.0005 rad (seq 3; 0.05 % of its range), 0.25 (seq 7; 0.6 %)
and 0.22 (seq 22; 3 %). Seq 7 and 22 sit near the Hopf point, where perturbations decay slowly and five periods are not
quite enough. The delay correction recovers the ground truth to 0.011, 1.4 and 0.004 rad respectively. The
quarter-period delay map is ill-conditioned over much of the cycle and is masked there.

**The coordinate caveat.** The "corrected" curve assumes the hidden coordinate held fixed during a nudge is the
model's w. Any other hidden coordinate gives another curve from the same voltage data (Fig. 1 style, right panel): the
constant-delay PRC differs from the w-fixed one by 12 (seq 3), 300 (seq 7) and 56 (seq 22) rad per model unit at worst.
Without a perturbation experiment, and without knowing what a fungal "gating variable" is, there is no basis for
choosing one.

`seq<N>_delay`: the **recorded** cycles in delay coordinates (V(t), V(t − δ)) at the paper's delay mapped to seconds
(24 s, 1 s, 0.1 s; the last two rounded to one sample) and at a quarter cycle, with the fitted loop over them.

## Reproducing the paper

The paper's FHN gives T = 11.2279 (Appendix B's guess is 3 × 3.743 = 11.229). In eq. 17's own (v, w) its voltage PRC
runs −3.24 to 1.34; along the axis its figure code plots (centred on the fixed point, rotated −9.5°) it runs −2.15 to
2.60, matching Fig. 4's −2.2 to 2.6. Both are pinned as tests.

## What this can and cannot support

* **Can:** "The mean drop cycle of these oyster sequences is better described by a single kicked excursion than by a
  limit cycle, and better still by a fall plus a smooth recovery" — as a ranking of models on three sequences.
* **Can:** "The PRC of any fitted voltage model depends on the hidden coordinate assumed constant", demonstrated on
  fitted models, with the paper's correction reproduced.
* **Cannot:** that fungal cycles *are* FHN-like, excitable or oscillatory as biology: the fit quality does not beat
  noise on the oyster sequences, and parameters sit on bounds.
* **Cannot:** any measured phase response. There were no perturbation experiments.
* **Cannot:** a spiking-family taxonomy from three mean cycles. A family claim would need per-cycle fits across many
  sequences, a shape statistic that noise does not match, and ideally a stimulus.

## Caveats

1. Means of jittered cycles smear sharp features; the reishi mean (20 samples per cycle) is nearly a sine wave.
2. Seq 3 includes one cycle with a +38 mV spike artefact; the mean carries a trace of it.
3. Null draws are few (10 / 5); "beats every draw" is a p ≲ 0.1–0.2 statement, not stronger.
4. The quiet null for seq 3 had only 94 candidate windows, drawn with replacement.
5. The analysis code lives in `Pipelines/fhn/` rather than `Working/`, because `Working/` was out of scope for this job.
