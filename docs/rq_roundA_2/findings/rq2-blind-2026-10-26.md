# RQ2, blind: seed matches against random windows (2026-10-26)

## In plain words

The seed searches look very good because the researcher accepted almost everything they found, but every match was judged knowing it was a match. Two things here make that a fair test. First, the null: how many matches a scrambled recording gives at the same cut, which needs no judging. Second, a blind panel: seed matches, earlier-accepted matches and random windows of the same lengths, shuffled into one queue that does not say which is which. If the seed's matches are accepted far more often than the random windows, the seed finds something real; if earlier yeses survive the blind look, the unblinded rates were not inflated.

Made by `scripts/rq2_blind.py` (see its docstring for the commands). Read with `docs/rq_roundA/RQ2-exemplar-seeded-search.md` and `docs/rq_roundA_2/RQ2b-second-seed.md`.

## The null at each seed's cut (needs no judging)

In plain words: for each seed the search was also run 200 times on scrambled copies of each channel (phase randomisation keeps a channel's spectrum and destroys its shapes). If the real channel gives many more matches under the cut than a scrambled copy does, the matches are not what chance gives. "Null mean" is the average number of scrambled matches per draw at the same line; "p95" is the 95th percentile over the 200 draws.

Cut rule: α = 0.01 per null draw, correction: none (Settings › Nulls). **Line** is the one cut the Seed page draws (the strictest passing channel's); **own** is each channel's own cut; **judged** is the cut the earlier, unblinded judging used.
