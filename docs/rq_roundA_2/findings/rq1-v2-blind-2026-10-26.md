## In plain words

A computer was taught to sort ten-minute stretches of signal using only groups that a clustering algorithm made, with
no human answers at all. The researcher then labelled 1,000 stretches the computer had never seen, without seeing its
answer. The question: does the computer's "interesting" match the researcher's?

It is like a smoke alarm that goes off at nine out of ten real fires, but also every time someone makes toast. The
forest finds about **nine in ten of the windows the researcher calls interesting (recall 0.91–0.93)**, but only about
**one in four of its alarms is one the researcher agrees with (precision 0.26–0.30)**, because it calls about 70 % of
all windows interesting. It is clearly better than chance (it beats every one of 1,000 shuffles), but it is much
worse than the old forest trained on human labels, which raises fewer alarms and is right about nine in ten of them.

Read: on macro F1 (the primary score fixed before the baseline run), arm A beats B.2 on every subset with enough
interesting windows, and the interval excludes zero. On **balanced accuracy the two are close** (0.66 vs 0.64 on
windows with no earlier label), because they fail in opposite directions: arm A misses two in three interesting
windows (recall 0.31–0.41) at high precision; B.2 finds nine in ten at low precision. On the truly unseen recording
(M2_concat) arm A's recall collapses (2 of 13). Both arms' null p-values are at their floors except on M2_concat.

## What it means for RQ1

- Cluster labels alone produce a classifier that is **better than chance on unseen channels but not better than
  manual labels**: it trades precision for recall. "Better generalisation" in the PRD's sense is not shown.
- The researcher's mapping (5 of 7 clusters → interesting) is the main reason for the low precision: most clusters
  mapped interesting are 64–86 % *not* interesting to the blind eye. A stricter mapping (only cluster 7, the most
  interesting at 36 %) is a different, post-hoc model and must not be scored on these labels.
- The manual-label forest's 0.79 (run 79's exam (ii), its own labels) falls to 0.69–0.70 macro F1 on blind labels
  of windows with no earlier label, and 0.59 on M2_concat: its 0.79 was partly in-sample.

Exam (iii), M4, was not touched.
